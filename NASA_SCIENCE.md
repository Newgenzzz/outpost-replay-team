# Science sources and training model

Outpost Replay runs a fictional nine-day exercise near the rim of Shackleton crater. The game bundles selected published values and a scripted scenario in `src/data/nasaTelemetry.ts`. Gameplay runs offline; the optional DONKI flare catalog requires network access. The science panel separates published observations, estimates derived from observations, engineering references, and training assumptions.

## Scientific boundaries verified for M1 · 8 November 2026

### Procedural 3D illustration versus NASA terrain

`src/data/shackletonDem.ts` creates a bowl, peaks, ridges and roughness with authored mathematical functions. **It contains no LOLA elevation samples or Diviner thermal map.** Its coordinates and elevation zero are arbitrary; S/C1 markers are illustrative, not mapped to the published study locations. Colors do not establish temperature, composition or detected ice. The previous 88 K floor label and 86% / 89.7% site claims were unsupported and have been removed.

The existing scene and teaching calculation are retained. The nominal 36 km patch and 4.2 km bowl parameter describe the illustration. Zuber et al. (2012), Table 1, reports a mean rim diameter of about 21 km and mean depth of 4.1 ± 0.05 km from LOLA topography; this is a separate published result, not a fit of our mesh. [NASA NTRS manuscript](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120013758/downloads/20120013758.pdf)

The horizon demonstration samples the procedural function along a ray at a fixed 1.55° Sun elevation. Its observer-height effect is deliberately multiplied by **14**; the rendered mast and lighting are also illustrative. The percentage is a linear limb-height visibility index, not the area of a physically integrated solar disk, annual illumination, or a verified 2 m versus 10 m power forecast. The interface states these limits beside the scene. This independent visualization does not drive mission resources. A genuine DEM is unnecessary for this limited educational demonstration, so no terrain dataset has been downloaded or integrated.

LOLA is an actual laser-ranging instrument; its observations support research terrain and illumination models. The published S/C1 planner uses the separate Table 2 values below. M1 rechecked the table: **85.5% and 88.0% mean visible disk**, **66 h and 112 h maximum shadow**, at **2 m** over **2017–2037**. These are neither percentages measured by our hologram nor a promise of continuous light at every rim point. [NASA instrument description](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lro/lola/) · [Gläser et al., Table 2, manuscript p. 12](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170007365/downloads/20170007365.pdf#page=12)

### GOES observations, DONKI flare metadata and classroom doses

- **Observed near Earth:** NOAA PRF 2541, p. 1, reports the ≥10 MeV GOES proton event starting at 13:35 UTC on 10 May 2024, peaking at **207 pfu at 17:45 UTC**, associated with an **X3.9** flare. The prior X8.7/G5 label conflated separate flare/geomagnetic information with this particle event. A pfu is proton·cm⁻²·s⁻¹·sr⁻¹. These are not measurements at our lunar base. [NOAA report](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/stp/space-weather/swpc-products/weekly_reports/PRFs_of_SGD/2024/05/prf2541.pdf) · [NOAA radiation-storm definition](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/phenomena/solar-radiation-storm)
- **Catalog context:** DONKI distinguishes solar-flare (FLR) and solar energetic particle (SEP) records. The optional FLR query displays returned X-ray flare class, timestamp and official record link only. It does not derive pfu or dose, change the selected training peak, or rewrite either run. Failed, empty and successful queries have distinct statuses; no invented archive record replaces an unavailable live response. Gameplay remains available with bundled training inputs. NASA describes DONKI products as research/prototyping information. [NASA DONKI description and rules](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/) · [official API documentation](https://api.nasa.gov/) · [September 2026 URL migration notice](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/news/major-updates/)
- **Assumed alternatives:** The **840 pfu** and **18 pfu** choices retain their amplitudes and internal IDs for replay compatibility. They are explicitly training assumptions. The former is not verified as the September 2017 event peak; the latter has no supported event date, active region or flare class. Eighteen pfu is above NOAA's near-Earth 10 pfu storm threshold and is not labeled a quiet observation.
- **Educational calculation:** The engine's fictional 48-hour pulse, **0.015 mSv·h⁻¹·pfu⁻¹** coefficient, shielding transmission factors, 1 mSv/day background and 50 mSv warning remain unchanged. The coefficient is not calibrated radiation transport. Real exposure depends on energy spectra, material/geometry, secondary particles and biological weighting. The unshielded storm-only integral is approximately **46.8 mSv at the default 207 pfu**; the habitat factor 0.25 gives about **11.7 mSv of storm-only dose**, before background, other exposure and any station modifier. The incorrect ~46 mSv habitat description was removed. Forecasts and evidence charts now identify the active peak; static explanations avoid presenting default doses as universal.
- **Standards:** NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 1 Rev. C, §4.8.2, requires career effective spaceflight dose below **600 mSv** and §4.8.1 requires ALARA. The game's modeled mSv values cannot establish compliance with this or other NASA exposure limits; 50 mSv is a training rule, not a NASA mission standard. Active Rev. C metadata and the source PDF were checked. [Official standard, PDF p. 40](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/C//nasa-std-3001-vol-1-rev-c-signature.pdf#page=40)

### Historical comparisons and units

The former May 2024 ISS “OSDR RadLab” trace and lunar μGy/h curve had no dataset identifier, extraction method or verifiable observations. Both series and the unverified ISS peak values have been removed. Their chart area now plots **classroom mSv/h estimates from the same scripted pulse used by the mission**, with assumed 100% and 25% transmission, and explicitly says it is not ISS/Moon telemetry.

For a genuine historical example, NASA's September 2017 Mars graphic distinguishes MAVEN proton measurements in specified energy bands from Curiosity RAD **absorbed dose in μGy/day**. It shows why atmosphere and particle energy matter; its numbers are not imported or rescaled into our lunar game. Different locations, detectors, shielding, spectra and time intervals prevent a direct “ISS versus Moon” dose ratio. **Gy** is absorbed energy per mass; **Sv** incorporates radiation-quality weighting (and, for effective dose, tissue weighting). They are not interchangeable labels or a universal unit conversion. [NASA Mars graphic and measurement description](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/solar-storms-radiation-at-martian-orbit-and-surface/) · [NASA Space Math: Dose and Dose Equivalent](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Radiation_Math.pdf)

The fictional base has no verified coordinates; launch and certificate labels no longer assign it 89.9° S. D2's seal bonus is described as **4 percentage points during D2**, subject to power availability, rather than an unsupported 94→98% permanent hardware improvement. The storm clamp reduces only sheltered storm exposure in the model, not EVA or background. No resource equations, gameplay effects or renderer settings were changed for these corrections.

## Lunar Site Intelligence

D1 and the Sunlight tab contain a battery sizing exercise for two lunar candidate locations. The game bundles two rows transcribed from Table 2 of Gläser et al. (2018), available through NASA NTRS with public use permitted. The study used 20 m/pixel lunar terrain derived from LRO LOLA measurements. It modeled illumination hourly from 1 January 2017 to 1 January 2037 at 2 m above local ground. [Accepted manuscript and distribution record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20170007365)

| Study point | Latitude (°) | Longitude (° as published) | Mean visible solar disk | Longest modeled shadow |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Shackleton rim (S, first Shackleton row) | −89.6866 | 197.19 | 85.5% | 66 h |
| Connecting Ridge (C1) | −89.4398 | 222.83 | 88.0% | 112 h |

The reproducible extract is `src/data/lunarSiteIntelligence.ts`. Read the site, coordinates, maximum average illumination, and maximum time in shadow columns for those two rows in Table 2. Each row represents a particular study point. The mean illumination averages the visible fraction of the solar disk at each hourly sample and differs from the fraction of hours with any sunlight. The extract uses the published numbers as given, with no raster conversion or geospatial transformation. The fictional outpost has no assigned study coordinates.

The planning calculator reuses the exercise’s 12 kWh/day full-light generation and 10 kWh/day base load. It estimates long-term mean generation by multiplying 12 kWh/day by the mean visible fraction, and estimates the energy needed through the longest shadow by multiplying the constant load by the shadow duration in Earth days. It assumes a Sun-tracking array with output proportional to visible disk fraction, zero solar output in shadow, and no heater draw or storage losses. For a 35 kWh usable reserve, Shackleton's 27.5 kWh shadow demand leaves 7.5 kWh while C1's 46.7 kWh demand leaves an 11.7 kWh shortfall. C1 still has the higher mean generation estimate, 10.56 versus 10.26 kWh/day. These are separate planning measures. The mission’s hourly simulation uses its own scripted conditions.

NASA PGDA Product 69 provides separate surface illumination rasters averaged over a lunar nutation cycle. A NASA SVS animation illustrates how shadows move and the LROC WAC product provides a separate imaging-based perspective at 100 m/pixel. They provide context and were not used to derive the two table rows. [NASA PGDA Product 69](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/69) · [NASA SVS south-polar illumination](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4930) · [LROC WAC south-polar illumination product](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/view_rdr/WAC_POLE_ILL_PCT_SOUTH)

## Training assumptions

- **Sunlight and temperature.** The mission uses a fixed 216-hour schedule sampled every 12 hours, with 72 hours of reduced sunlight at hours 48–120. Low panels use 15% of nominal array output in shadow, an elevated mast uses 40%, and panel alignment applies a cosine factor. The 90 K minimum is a training surface temperature. The heater term represents the increased electrical demand of a cold environment without solving a thermal model.
- **Electrical power.** Hourly energy accounting draws from solar generation, backup supply, and a finite battery. Essential habitat loads are served first. Engineering tasks and greenhouse lights depend on available power. The 12 kWh/day nominal solar supply, 10 kWh/day baseline load, and 100 kWh battery are chosen for classroom gameplay.
- **Solar calibration.** Completing the optional D1 azimuth exercise unlocks a calibration setting for that run. When applied, the training model caps the array’s incidence angle at 4° and retains the original angle if it is already smaller. The cosine of that angle scales generation while terrain visibility and the shadow schedule stay fixed. The 128° practice azimuth and 4° modeled incidence cap represent different quantities. Run B inherits the setting and can revise it when rewinding D1. The judge preset leaves calibration off.
- **Life support and water.** Stores track oxygen, water, and food in kilograms. Recycling reduces net water draw and becomes less effective under power stress. The D4 ice rig uses planned feed mass × 0.056 × extraction efficiency × the fraction of task power delivered. The 5.6% fraction comes from the LCROSS estimate for Cabeus and serves as an analog for this fictional batch. At full power, the 20 kg batch yields about 0.67 kg of treated water at the standard 60% efficiency or 0.84 kg at the prepared 75% efficiency. Both efficiencies and the improvement from preparation are classroom assumptions. The station exercise unlocks the preparation setting, and only applying it with D4 option A changes the yield. Rewind records that setting so the two runs can isolate its effect. Gross water demand and outage recovery are also exercise assumptions. The life-support meter combines equipment health with a two-day reserve target.
- **Food.** Stored rations supply the mission. A pre-grown tray enters at a modeled age of 25 days. It reaches harvest at 30 powered growing days while using a small daily water and energy budget. The modeled harvest and ration-equivalent credit illustrate a supplement from fresh produce.
- **Radiation.** The fictional 48-hour lunar storm borrows only the 207 pfu peak amplitude from the GOES event near Earth. Its timing and shape are scripted. The engine integrates the scripted flux with an assumed coefficient of 0.015 mSv/h per pfu and assumed transmission of 100% for the suit, 25% for the habitat, 15% for a water wall, or 6% for the berm. Background adds an assumed 1 mSv/day. The resulting mSv values are classroom estimates for comparing choices. A real crew-dose prediction requires particle spectra, transport through the actual shielding, and biological weighting. NASA-STD-3001’s 600 mSv career effective-dose limit is a separate reference. The game’s 50 mSv warning is a scenario rule.
- **Replay.** Each run records an immutable storm scenario and complete task settings with every applied choice. Field discoveries remain drafts until Apply and do not rewrite earlier phases. Cumulative sample counts earn only newly applied credit. A rewound branch retains all other phase snapshots. The guided demo shares the same starting inventories, environment and preparations; imported runs with different storm peaks are labeled as different setups. If a run aborts, its history stops at that mission hour. Final inventory deltas require equal elapsed times. Version 2 replay links carry both complete inputs; legacy links warn that omitted preparation settings use defaults.
- **Prediction exercise.** The optional D3 prediction compares the recorded battery reserves at hour 120 for backup fuel cells and drilling. It requires the same D1/D2 choices, effective calibration, ECLSS, bus and field-sample inputs through D3, and the same storm scenario. Both D3 records must reach hour 120. Later mission plans and endpoints may differ; this does not permit comparing unequal final inventories. Reset clears the prediction. The judge demonstration skips this optional activity.

## Where to verify

Facility readouts visualize the selected simulation snapshot. The shade layer follows the scripted sunlight, crop trays follow modeled growth and harvest, and the processor displays the calculated treated-water yield. Short equipment outlines acknowledge modeled state changes; unlocking preparation alone does not add water. Survey flags identify fictional icy-soil sampling points. These graphics illustrate the exercise and its results.

The optional ridge investigation reuses the fictional Laser Altimetry Marker and its existing field-point award. Logging it on the map unlocks a question about local terrain, the visible horizon, and illumination. It collects no real altimetry or solar measurement. The explanation links to the existing NASA LOLA reference and the Sunlight planner. The planner's **Trace the evidence** controls distinguish laser return-time measurements from the research illumination models and the exercise's own load, storage, and lighting assumptions.

Open **Science & Sources** in the game to see how the published values relate to the scenario. The end-of-mission Evidence panel lists the training equations and source links. The code separates the power budget, resource ledger, and deterministic mission calculation under `src/engine/`.

## M3 engineering accounting clarification

The five collectible cores each add an assumed 3 kg of icy regolith to D4-A (up to 35 kg total feed), not 3 kg of pure ice. At full task power, five cores yield 1.176 kg of treated water at 60% recovery or 1.47 kg at 75%; lower delivered power reduces yield. All five now participate in the calculation. Mission scoring remains a classroom weighting with an explicit 0–100 bound, and hourly science charts show committed phase awards rather than provisional points. No NASA physical parameter or radiation assumption was changed for M3.
